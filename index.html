简介About

关于我About Me

我是一名动物生态学研究者，主要关注鸟类繁殖、亲代抚育与胚胎发育，以及温度变化如何影响这些过程。我尤其感兴趣的是鸟类如何通过孵卵行为和亲代投入调节胚胎所处的热环境，以及不同繁殖策略和生态环境如何塑造这种温度响应。 我目前就读于北京林业大学自然保护区学专业，从事动物生态学方向的博士研究。我的研究结合野外观察、行为生态学、比较生理学和 Meta 分析等方法，从个体行为到跨物种比较，探索温度、繁殖策略与动物适应之间的关系。 目前，我在澳大利亚国立大学开展联合培养研究，在 Dr. Daniel Noble 的指导下学习并应用 Meta 分析方法，研究温度对鸟类胚胎发育的影响。 我希望理解动物如何通过行为、生理和繁殖策略应对不断变化的环境，以及这些机制如何塑造动物的生态与演化。 I am an animal ecologist interested in avian reproduction, parental care, and embryonic development, with a particular focus on how temperature shapes these processes. I am especially interested in how birds regulate the thermal environment experienced by developing embryos through incubation behaviour and parental investment, and how reproductive strategies and ecological conditions shape these thermal responses. I am currently pursuing my PhD in Nature Reserve Science at Beijing Forestry University, with a focus on animal ecology. My research combines field observations, behavioural ecology, comparative physiology, and meta-analysis to explore the relationships among temperature, reproductive strategies, and animal adaptation across individual and comparative scales. I am currently undertaking a joint PhD research placement at the Australian National University under the supervision of Dr. Daniel Noble, where I am learning and applying meta-analysis approaches to investigate the effects of temperature on avian embryonic development. More broadly, I am interested in how animals respond to changing environments through behavioural, physiological, and reproductive strategies, and how these mechanisms shape ecological and evolutionary diversity.

研究方向Research Focus

"我研究环境变化如何塑造鸟类的发育、行为与生活史，以及这些响应背后的生态与进化机制。" "I study how environmental variation shapes avian development, behaviour, and life histories, and the ecological and evolutionary mechanisms underlying these responses."

我的研究主要关注环境变化如何影响鸟类的繁殖、亲代抚育和早期发育，以及鸟类如何通过行为和生理调节应对环境变化。我尤其关注孵卵行为如何塑造胚胎的热环境，以及不同孵卵模式、巢结构和生态背景如何影响胚胎对温度变化的响应。通过野外观察、实验研究、比较分析和系统综述与Meta分析，我尝试从生态和进化的角度理解亲代行为、发育可塑性和生活史之间的联系，并进一步探讨环境变化和气候变暖对鸟类繁殖与发育的潜在影响。 My research focuses on how environmental variation influences avian reproduction, parental care, and early development, and how birds respond to environmental challenges through behavioural and physiological mechanisms. I am particularly interested in how incubation behaviour shapes the thermal environment experienced by embryos, and how incubation mode, nest structure, and ecological context influence developmental responses to temperature. Using field observations, experiments, comparative analyses, and systematic reviews and meta-analyses, I aim to understand the ecological and evolutionary links among parental behaviour, developmental plasticity, and life-history variation, and to explore the potential consequences of environmental change and climate warming for avian reproduction and development.

01 · TOPIC

繁殖与亲代抚育Avian Reproduction & Parental Care

研究鸟类如何通过繁殖策略、亲代投入与孵卵行为调节繁殖过程，探索环境条件如何塑造不同的亲代抚育策略。 Studying how birds regulate reproduction through reproductive strategies, parental investment, and incubation behaviour, and how environmental conditions shape parental care strategies.

02 · TOPIC

孵卵行为与热生态学Incubation Behaviour & Thermal Ecology

研究鸟类如何通过孵卵行为调节卵的热环境，以及温度如何影响孵卵投入、孵卵模式与繁殖过程。 Studying how birds regulate the thermal environment of developing embryos through incubation behaviour, and how temperature influences incubation investment, incubation strategies, and reproduction.

03 · TOPIC

胚胎发育与发育可塑性Embryonic Development & Developmental Plasticity

研究温度等早期环境因素如何影响鸟类胚胎发育、表型和生存，以及这些发育响应如何塑造个体对环境变化的适应。 Studying how temperature and other early-life environmental conditions shape embryonic development, phenotypes, and survival, and how developmental responses contribute to adaptation to environmental change.

04 · TOPIC

比较生理学与演化生态学Comparative Physiology & Evolutionary Ecology

从跨物种和演化尺度比较动物对环境变化的生理与行为响应，探索繁殖策略、生态环境与进化历史如何共同塑造动物的适应。 Using comparative and evolutionary approaches to investigate how physiological and behavioural responses to environmental variation are shaped by reproductive strategies, ecological conditions, and evolutionary history.
